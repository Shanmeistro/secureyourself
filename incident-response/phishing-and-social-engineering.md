# Phishing and Social Engineering

[← Incident response index](README.md)

## 1. Trigger
- I clicked a link, opened an attachment, entered credentials, or shared a code
- Someone is pressuring me by phone/message to act urgently or pay

## 2. Immediate priorities
1. Stop engaging.
2. Assume anything entered is compromised.
3. Secure accounts before reporting.

## 3. First 15 minutes
1. Disconnect from the call/chat.
2. If credentials were entered: change that password and any reuse, from a trusted device.
3. If a code was shared: sign out sessions and reset.
4. If an attachment was opened or software installed: disconnect the device from the network.

## 4. First 24 hours
1. Check account activity ([account compromise](account-compromise.md) if anything unexpected).
2. If money was sent: contact the bank immediately; ask about reversal.
3. Run an update/malware scan on the device; reset if unsure.
4. Report the message to the impersonated brand and email/platform provider.

## 5. Recovery and verification
- Sign-ins and MFA methods for affected accounts are reviewed and clean
- Device scans clean or device reset
- No new rules/apps; alerts on

## 6. Evidence to preserve
- Screenshots of the message, header details, phone number/sender ID (off-repo)
- Don't open links again to 'capture' them

## 7. Who to contact
- Bank/card issuer; platform or email provider
- Employer if a work account/device is involved
- Local fraud reporting service (`[LOCAL REPORTING SERVICE]`)

## 8. What not to do
- Don't click again to 'check'
- Don't call numbers in the message; use official channels
- Don't be embarrassed into silence; speed matters
- Don't pay or buy gift cards

## 9. After-action review
What made it convincing? Which control (autofill, MFA type) did or didn't help? Update [email](../communications/email.md) habits.
